The normal human body temperature for a healthy adult typically ranges between **36.1°C and 37.2°C** (97°F to 99°F).

While **37°C** (98.6°F) is commonly cited as the standard average, body temperature naturally fluctuates throughout the day and varies depending on where and how it is measured:

| Measurement Method | Normal Range (°C) |
| --- | --- |
| **Oral** (mouth) | 36.3°C – 37.2°C |
| **Tympanic** (ear) | 36.4°C – 37.5°C |
| **Axillary** (armpit) | 35.9°C – 36.7°C |
| **Rectal** (core) | 36.6°C – 38.0°C |

**Key Factors That Influence Temperature:**

* **Time of Day:** Temperature is typically lowest in the early morning (around 4:00 AM) and highest in the late afternoon or early evening (around 4:00 PM to 6:00 PM).
* **Age:** Older adults tend to have lower baseline body temperatures than younger adults and children.
* **Activity & Environment:** Exercise, hot weather, high humidity, and warm clothing can temporarily raise body temperature.

Medical professionals generally consider a body temperature of **38°C (100.4°F) or higher** to be a fever.

=== Q20 ===
The main purpose of washing hands with soap is to **remove and destroy germs (bacteria, viruses, and parasites), dirt, and oils** from your skin to prevent the spread of infections and disease.

Water alone can rinse away loose dirt, but it cannot effectively break down oils or trap microorganisms. Soap accomplishes this through a specific chemical mechanism:

* **Breaks down viral and bacterial structures:** Soap molecules have a dual end—one side attracts water (hydrophilic) and the other attracts lipids/fats (hydrophobic). Many pathogens, including respiratory viruses, are encased in a fatty lipid membrane. Soap molecules wedge themselves into this membrane and tear it apart, destroying the virus or bacterium.
* **Lifts pathogens from the skin:** The friction created by rubbing your hands together with soap lather traps dirt, grease, and micro-organisms inside soap structures called **micelles**, allowing water to wash them cleanly down the drain.

### Key Moments to Wash Hands

* Before eating or preparing food
* After using the bathroom or changing diapers
* After coughing, sneezing, or blowing your nose
* After touching animals, animal feed, or animal waste
* After handling garbage or touching frequently used public surfaces

Proper handwashing requires scrubbing all surfaces of your hands—including the backs, between fingers, and under nails—for at least **20 seconds**.

$$0.25\text{ hours} \times 60\text{ minutes/hour} = 15\text{ minutes}$$

=== Q28 ===
The sky looks blue because of a phenomenon called **Rayleigh scattering**, which describes how sunlight interacts with the gases in Earth's atmosphere.

---

### Key Mechanisms

1. **Sunlight contains all colors:** Sunlight appears white to us, but it is actually made up of all the colors of the visible spectrum combined.
2. **Wavelengths differ:** Light travels in waves. Red and orange light have long, stretched-out wavelengths, while blue and violet light travel in short, smaller wavelengths.
3. **Atmospheric gas scattering:** Earth's atmosphere is full of tiny molecules of nitrogen and oxygen. When white sunlight hits these gas molecules, the short blue wavelengths bump into them and scatter in every direction much more effectively than the longer red wavelengths.
4. **Human eye perception:** Although violet light has an even shorter wavelength and scatters more than blue, human eyes are far more sensitive to blue light. Additionally, the sun emits significantly more blue light than violet.

(gemini also showed image)

### Why the Sky Turns Red and Orange at Sunset

When the sun sets, light must travel through a much thicker layer of atmosphere to reach your eyes. By the time the sunlight reaches you, almost all the blue light has already been scattered away, allowing the longer red, orange, and yellow wavelengths to pass directly through without being scattered out of sight.

=== Q29 ===
```python
# Problem: 5 machines take 5 minutes to make 5 widgets.
# Rate per machine:
# 5 machines make 5 widgets in 5 minutes -> 1 machine makes 1 widget in 5 minutes.
# So 1 machine rate = 1 widget per 5 minutes = 1/5 widgets per minute.

# For 100 machines making 100 widgets:
# 100 machines make widgets at a combined rate of 100 * (1/5) = 20 widgets per minute.
# Time required for 100 widgets = 100 widgets / 20 widgets per minute = 5 minutes.

machines_initial = 5
time_initial = 5
widgets_initial = 5

rate_per_machine = (widgets_initial / machines_initial) / time_initial # widgets per machine per minute

machines_target = 100
widgets_target = 100

combined_rate = machines_target * rate_per_machine
time_target = widgets_target / combined_rate

print(f"Time target: {time_target} minutes")


```

```text
Time target: 5.0 minutes


```

It will take **5 minutes**.

Here is why:

1. **Individual Machine Rate:** If 5 machines produce 5 widgets in 5 minutes, that means each machine produces **1 widget every 5 minutes**.
2. **Scaling Up:** If you put 100 machines to work at the exact same time, each machine will still take 5 minutes to complete its own widget.
3. **Total Production:** After 5 minutes, all 100 machines will finish their 1 widget simultaneously, giving you a total of 100 widgets.

The **pancreas** produces insulin. Specifically, specialized clusters of cells within the pancreas called **beta cells** (located in the islets of Langerhans) synthesize and secrete the hormone into the bloodstream to regulate blood sugar levels.
(gemini also showed image)
A lack of insulin results in **Diabetes Mellitus**:

* **Type 1 Diabetes:** An autoimmune condition where the immune system destroys the pancreas's beta cells, resulting in little to no insulin production.
* **Type 2 Diabetes:** Occurs when the body becomes resistant to insulin, and over time, the pancreas fails to produce enough to meet the body's needs.

Without sufficient insulin, glucose cannot enter cells to be used for energy, leading to high blood sugar levels (**hyperglycemia**).
